IMIT OVR Playbook

The loop

Edge (a sweep fails) → Control (Reclaim Hold) → Acceptance (Control Defense) → Delivery (Displacement) → Exhaustion → Reset (Compression, Balance or Completion).

Reading order, every time

1 Structure: which state is active. 2 Control: who holds the auction. 3 Confirmation: is participation backing the move. 4 Risk: do conditions support it.

A setup needs all four

Control confirmed by a Reclaim Hold or Control Defense. An unfilled imbalance in the path. The sequence intact, not in Exhaustion. Risk conditions supporting: session window, distance from control, key levels.

Size by tier

Tier 3 or below: observe only. Tier 4: one entry at reduced size. Tier 5: full size, and only when all four elements agree. The farther price is from the Reclaim Hold, the smaller the size. At Exhaustion, size is zero. The 9am and 3pm windows carry full weight, 6pm is reduced, overnight is Tier 3 at best. One imbalance target at a time.

Managing the trade

In Delivery, do not add and do not tighten into the body. Move the stop to the latest Control Defense. Displacement is not an entry; wait for the retest. Retaliation that closes in your direction means the thesis is intact. Near Exhaustion, no adds, tighten, and consider a partial exit. If delivery fails mid-arc, exit, reset bias to neutral and read the new sequence.

All 19 states

Bullish, buyers in control: Sweep, Reclaim Hold, Control Defense, Delivery, Displacement, Retaliation, Exhaustion.

Bearish, sellers in control: the same seven, mirrored.

Neutral, control contested: Compression, Balance, Indecision, Completion.

Do not trade the sweep. Bias moves at the Reclaim Hold, not before. Delivery is management, not entry. Do not fade Displacement. Retaliation means the thesis is intact. Exhaustion means no new entries.

The five sequences

1 Bullish: Sweep, Reclaim Hold, Control Defense, Delivery, Displacement, Retaliation, Exhaustion.

2 Bearish: the mirror of the bullish arc.

3 Trap: a sweep, no reclaim, failed participation, then exhaustion or reset. Often the setup for the opposite direction. Most reactive losses come from trading the sweep as if it were a reclaim.

4 Trend continuation: Control Defense, Displacement, Delivery. No sweep needed because the regime is already set. One of the highest-confidence continuation reads.

5 End of move: Delivery, Exhaustion, then Compression, Balance or Completion. Stop pressing and wait for the next chapter.

When control shifts

Bull Sweep then Bear Reclaim Hold (or the mirror): trap sequence, drop the old bias at once. Bull or Bear Exhaustion then Compression: wait. Compression then a Reclaim Hold either way: the new regime has begun and bias moves there. Bull Delivery then Bear Sweep (or the mirror): a full trap, the highest-probability setup for the other side. Bull Control Defense then Bear Reclaim Hold (or the mirror): the defense failed and the old thesis is invalid. Completion then Compression: the natural reset. Balance then Displacement either way: a high-confidence breakout.

Disruption is information, not failure. Holding a bias through a confirmed disruption is denial.

Operator discipline

Observation. Read the state before anything else. The four-layer order is a loop that restarts on every state change or disruption. Waiting is an action. Compression and balance say wait. The first twenty minutes of the 9am session is a diagnostic: initiative or responsive, carried from the prior session, balanced or directional.

Ten core values. Structural: state before setup, sequence awareness, disruption recognition. Process: tier before size, entry from confirmation not anticipation, exit from structure not emotion, process audit over result audit. Identity: identity before strategy, the market as teacher, the mentorless deserve structure.

The path. Reactor, Seeker, Narrator, Structural Perceiver, Operator, Fund Manager, Cartographer. Stage is set by behavior under pressure, not years or account size.
